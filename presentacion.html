2. Uses and Purposes of Aerospace Technology

2 / 7

We can divide this field into two main areas:

  • Aeronautics (Flight inside the atmosphere): Commercial airplanes, helicopters, and drones. Goal: make transport faster, safer, and cheaper using light materials like carbon fiber.
  • Astronautics (Flight outside the atmosphere): Rockets, probes, and satellites for scientific research and global communications.

3. Aerospace Engineering in Our Daily Lives

3 / 7

Many objects we use were invented for space missions:

  • GPS: Satellites in orbit send high-precision signals to calculate our exact position using geometry and time equations.
  • NASA Spin-offs: Memory Foam (spaceship seats), smartphone cameras (space probes), and home water filters (Apollo missions).

4. Practical Demonstration: The 4 Forces

4 / 7

Any object in flight is controlled by four forces:

  • Thrust: The force that moves the object forward.
  • Drag: The air resistance that slows it down.
  • Weight: The gravity pulling the object to the ground.
  • Lift: The upward force created by the wings against weight.

5. Wing Geometry and Winglets

5 / 7

How geometry changes flight:

  • Curving the back of the wing upward pushes the tail down, making the nose go up (pitch effect).
  • Folding wing tips upward creates winglets, stopping large whirlpools (vortices) to keep flight stable and save millions in fuel.

6. The Rules in Space and Rockets

6 / 7

In space, there is no air, so drag and lift disappear and wings become useless.

  • Gravity & Free Fall: Satellites stay in orbit due to gravity; astronauts float because they are in continuous free fall.
  • Action and Reaction: Rockets move forward by pushing gas backward at supersonic speeds (Newton's Third Law).
  • Acceleration: Extreme speeds inside the atmosphere create friction and heat, requiring advanced ceramic materials.
